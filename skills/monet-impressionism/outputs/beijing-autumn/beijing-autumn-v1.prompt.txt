Oil painting in the manner of Claude Monet’s 1870s–1890s light studies: late October in Beijing, a stretch of vermilion palace wall beside a row of ginkgo trees in an old imperial park. It is about three or four in the afternoon; dry, high autumn sunlight slants from the right, passing through the golden canopy onto the mottled red wall and a carpet of fallen fan-shaped leaves. The sky is a clear, dry pale blue of 秋高气爽, slightly cooler and thinner near the horizon. A few small distant walkers are only quiet color notes. No full palace complex, no tourist postcard architecture — one wall, repeating trunks, leaves, and sky.

The same late-afternoon light organizes the picture: honey-yellow, ochre-gold, and a little remaining leaf-green carried by the ginkgo crowns and the ground carpet; sun-warmed vermilion and brick-red on the lit face of the wall, cooler violet-brown and deep umber in the wall’s shadow and the tree-trunk gaps; dry pale blue and a hint of green-blue in the sky seen through broken foliage; small concentrated notes of remaining green fans and warm earth on the path showing through the gold. Neighboring colors interweave as visible oil strokes; shadows take the wall’s violet-brown and the sky’s cool, not a gray veil or a golden filter laid over everything.

The ginkgo trunks are repeating vertical forms that catch different amounts of the same light, like a row of stacks. Canopy: clustered, overlapping strokes of yellow, ochre, olive, and violet-gray gaps — not every leaf drawn, not a grid of equal dots; a few near fallen leaves keep a readable fan shape. Ground: more scattered, slightly horizontal gold strokes with the gray stone path appearing in quieter gaps. Wall: more stable directional masonry strokes, edges lost where gold leaves overlap the vermilion and found again at the sunlit corner. Sky: broader, thinner layers of dry pale blue. Trunks: vertical, darker, with colored shadow. Selectively lost and found edges. No text, no signature, no water lilies, no Japanese bridge, no Forbidden City panorama.
