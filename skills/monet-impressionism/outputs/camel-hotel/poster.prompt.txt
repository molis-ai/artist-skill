A Claude Monet oil painting, about 1870–1900: a camel standing in front of a pale hotel in late afternoon slanting sun, treated like sunlit architecture and a haystack-like animal form, not a desert postcard.

The hotel facade on the right-rear is bone-white and cream-yellow where it faces the sun, with blue-violet and green-grey in the arched loggia and window recesses. The camel in the foreground is tawny ochre and warm gold on the lit humps, with colored blue-violet and olive shadows on the legs and belly. A wide ochre plaza recedes; long colored shadow of the camel stretches across the ground. Distant air cools toward blue-grey.

Large masses: sunlit plaza, the camel’s two humps as stable repeating forms in this one moment of light, the pale hotel block. Do not center both equally; camel slightly left, hotel behind to the right.

Brushwork: hotel walls as broader, more stable directional strokes, windows and arches only as much as needed for recognition, edges broken by light; camel body as interwoven neighboring strokes of ochre, rose-tan and violet, merge hair into color groups, keep short strokes for eye and muzzle; plaza as slanted earth strokes of yellow-ochre, pink-earth and cool grey with quiet gaps. Selectively lost and found edges between camel silhouette and hotel. No palm-tree clichés required, no water lilies, no signature, no text.

Working colors: milk-yellow, ochre-gold, blue-violet, green-grey, bone-white hotel, deep olive in the strongest shadow.
