Create one original horizontal 3:2 oil painting inspired by Claude Monet's outdoor painting of the 1890s. A quiet garden pond just after a summer shower, as midmorning sunlight begins to break through. Construct the entire scene from interwoven strokes of neighboring colors and selectively lost and found edges. At viewing distance the landscape is coherent; close up, the separate painterly decisions remain visible.

An intimate, off-center composition seen from the near bank. On the left, a small plain ivory-painted wooden garden chair stands empty on a narrow ochre gravel ledge beside the pond; it occupies a modest part of the painting. Its simple back and seat catch pale warm sunlight, with lavender-blue and reflected green in the shaded planes. The ledge enters diagonally from the lower left and ends at the water, making a clear contact between land and pond. The pond fills most of the center and right. A cropped willow enters from the upper right; loose hanging branches and their dark green reflections extend downward into the water. Across the upper portion, a densely planted far bank mixes warm yellow-greens with cooler blue-greens and a few muted rose and lilac flowers. There is only a small opening of pale sky above the far bank.

Make the pond's color structure the main experience: broad areas of cool green and blue-violet reflection alternate with a quieter patch of pale blue and warm cream reflected sky. Group a few pink-white water lilies and green floating pads into irregular, separated horizontal passages across this water, leaving substantial open intervals. The lily pads sit on the surface while tree reflections reach beneath them. Broken vertical traces of willow reflection are crossed and interrupted by horizontal water marks; the reflected sky is a softly interwoven area of paint, not a sharp mirrored photograph.

Use clustered, angled and drooping brushwork for foliage; varied horizontal dashes and longer quieter strokes for water; a few firmer directional strokes for the chair. Keep the large color masses legible beneath the smaller strokes. Let green, violet and warm ochre reappear in different proportions around the painting so the same light unifies the scene. Retain some deep green-violet passages and warm sunlit accents, rather than bleaching the whole painting. Oil color is layered, with some undercolor showing between strokes and occasional denser highlights. Paint thickness and stroke size vary by area. No uniformly thick palette-knife frosting, regular dots, swirling outlines, photographic detail under a texture filter, or artificial canvas-grid overlay. Full-bleed painting, no border, no text, no signature.
