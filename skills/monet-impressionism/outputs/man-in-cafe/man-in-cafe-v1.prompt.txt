Oil painting in the manner of Claude Monet’s 1870s–1890s light studies: a Paris café interior, late afternoon. A man in his thirties sits slightly left of center at a round white-marble café table, three-quarter view, lifting a small white cup of coffee; faint steam rises from the cup. Large windows at the right and rear admit low slanting sunlight from the street; stone window frames, a few bentwood chairs, and the table remain readable while sunlit dust and steam pass through the room.

The same late-afternoon light organizes the whole picture: cream-yellow and ochre-gold on the sunlit marble, window sills, cup rim, the man’s cheek, and the front of his pale shirt; cooler blue-violet and olive-gray in the far wall, under the table, the shadowed side of his dark jacket, and the street seen through glass; warm coffee-umber and deep brown in the coffee, chair gaps, and jacket folds; small concentrated notes of brick-red and warm flesh on a waistcoat edge, ear, and hands. Neighboring colors interweave as visible oil strokes rather than flat local color; shadows take on the interior’s violet and green-gray, not a gray veil.

Window frames and the table edge use more stable directional strokes with selectively lost and found edges. The man’s face, hands, and cup are a few interlocking color-shapes that keep pose, light, and contact readable without becoming a photographic portrait. Broader, thinner layers form the air and the broken street beyond the glass; local thicker dabs sit on the sunlit marble and steam. Repeated café chairs catch different amounts of the same light. No text, no signature, no water lilies, no Japanese bridge.
