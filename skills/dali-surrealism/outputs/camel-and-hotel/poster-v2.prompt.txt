Keep the same Salvador Dalí oil-painting language, cream hotel facade, camel head and neck, ochre desert, cool sky, distant rock, and wordless canvas. Change the scale and support: stretch the camel’s four legs into extremely long, thin, spider-like stilts with visible joints, so the body and hotel tower high against the sky and the legs fill much of the vertical space down to small two-toed feet on the sand. Make the hotel larger and heavier on the back; remove the separate pink saddle-plinth so the stone hotel sits directly on the hump, with a tight contact shadow proving the building’s weight is received there. The thin legs must look too fragile for the load, yet they meet the ground with pressure and throw long, simple late-afternoon shadows. Keep the camel recognizable (curved neck, small head, one hump). No extra figures, no hotel name, no signature.
