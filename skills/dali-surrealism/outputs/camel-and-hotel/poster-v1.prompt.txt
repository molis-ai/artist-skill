A Salvador Dalí-inspired oil painting of a single dromedary camel carrying an entire hotel on its back across a vast quiet plain. Precisely drawn transformed silhouettes, smooth sculptural tonal modeling, broad quiet painted planes. The camel remains recognizable by its long curved neck, small head, one hump, and two-toed feet, but its four legs are stretched into extremely long, thin, spider-like stilts that tower over the ground, so the body and hotel sit high against the sky. A compact grand hotel rests directly on the hump and back: a cream-and-ochre stone block with regular dark window recesses, a small entrance canopy and steps facing outward, and a quiet roofline; the building’s weight is clearly received by the camel’s back, with a tight contact shadow in the join. The thin legs meet the earth at small, precise hooves; pressure and long late-afternoon shadows make the improbable load feel heavy. The camel walks slowly toward the right, placed left of center, leaving a large empty ochre-and-bone desert that recedes to a low horizon with decreasing contrast; a tiny distant rock gives scale; no extra figures. Light from the left. Palette: large planes of ochre, yellow earth, and bone-white ground against a cool blue-grey sky with slow internal gradation; the camel’s sandy warm-brown body shares the ground’s family; the hotel’s cream walls take the light, with deepest darks in the window hollows, the under-hotel join, and the slender legs’ shadows in deep brown and blue-black. Fine description reserved for the camel’s head, the hotel-to-hump join, the feet on the ground, and a few window openings; desert, sky, and hotel wall planes stay quiet thin color layers. Wordless painting, no hotel name, no signature, no title, no extra faces, no religious procession, no elephants or obelisks.
