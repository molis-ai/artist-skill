A Salvador Dalí 1930s hallucinatory oil painting, not a product photo. Precisely drawn transformed silhouettes, smooth sculptural tonal modeling, broad quiet painted planes.

A classic contour glass cola bottle sits on a dry wooden table in an empty coastal plain. The bottle has lost rigidity: its glass body folds over the table’s front edge with continuous thickness, gravity, wrinkles and contact, yet remains recognizable as a cola bottle—hourglass contour, dark liquid inside, red script label still readable on the sagging glass. The table stays hard. A small metal bottle cap rests on the tabletop, slightly too far from the neck, casting a sharp shadow.

Low horizon, vast ochre-and-bone ground receding with slow tonal steps; sky cools from bone-white to blue-grey. Strong raking light from the left; a long simple shadow of the draped bottle. Fine detail only at the label, glass folds and cap; table, ground and sky stay broadly painted.

Working colors: ochre, yellow-earth, bone-white, blue-grey distance, deep brown in contact shadows, cola-dark and label-red as local identity colors. No extra title, no signature, no melting clocks, no extra faces.
