A Salvador Dalí-inspired oil painting of a single vintage 1930s automobile that has lost its rigidity at the exact center of a vast empty four-way crossroads. Precisely drawn transformed silhouettes, smooth sculptural tonal modeling, broad quiet painted planes. The car remains recognizable by a rounded grille, round headlamps, fenders, cabin and four wheels, but its metal body has gone soft with continuous thickness, folds and gravity: the roof and hood sag and drape over the crossing, one fender spreads onto a road, and the chassis rests on the intersection instead of sitting as a hard machine. A thin forked wooden crutch actually braces the heaviest sag of the roof at a small contact point, bearing the softened car’s weight. Two pale ochre roads form a clear cross on a bone-and-yellow earth plain, receding in four directions to a low horizon with decreasing contrast; the car sits on the join of the two roads, slightly left of center, leaving large empty ground and sky. Late-afternoon light from the left casts long simple shadows of the sagging car, the crutch and the cross of the roads. Palette: large planes of ochre, yellow earth and bone-white ground against a cool blue-grey sky with slow internal gradation; the car is a concentrated deep burgundy-red as the focal color, with cream and warm metal midtones; deepest darks in the undercarriage contact, window hollows and shadows, deep brown and blue-black. Fine description reserved for the grille and lamps, the metal-to-road folds, the crutch contact, and the exact crossing of the two roads; desert-like ground and sky remain quiet thin color layers. Wordless painting, no street names, no license-plate lettering, no traffic-sign text, no extra cars, no extra faces, no signature, no title.
