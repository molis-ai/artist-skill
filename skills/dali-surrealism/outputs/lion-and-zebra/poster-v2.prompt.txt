Keep the same Salvador Dalí oil-painting language, one animal, ochre desert, cool sky, distant rock, and wordless canvas. Change the join and the gaze. The lion’s mane must become the zebra: individual gold mane locks lengthen, flatten and darken into the first black-and-bone stripes, so tawny fur and stripes are one continuous flank under one light, not a lion head spliced onto a zebra body. Turn the lion head to look back along its own spine at the striped haunches. Slightly elongate the torso. Keep lion muzzle, whiskers, paws in front and zebra hooves and tufted tail behind. Broader sculptural planes on the body; fine description only at the eye, whiskers, and the mane-to-stripe join. No second animal, no hunt, no blood, no signature.
