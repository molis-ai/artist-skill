A Salvador Dalí-inspired oil painting of one continuous animal that is both lion and zebra, standing on a vast quiet plain. Precisely drawn transformed silhouettes, smooth sculptural tonal modeling, broad quiet painted planes. A single body: the front is a recognizable lion—mane, muzzle, whiskers, rounded ears, tawny paws—while along the shoulders and ribcage the same volume gradually becomes a zebra, tawny fur and radiating mane hair turning into black-and-bone stripes, the hindquarters a zebra with hooves and a tufted tail. The transition is one form under one light, not a splice of two animals and not a hunt. The lion head turns back to look at its own striped haunches. The creature stands slightly left of center, elongated but still one animal, leaving a large empty ochre-and-bone desert receding to a low horizon with decreasing contrast; a tiny distant rock gives scale; no extra figures. Late-afternoon light from the left; long simple shadows. Palette: large planes of ochre, yellow earth and bone-white ground against a cool blue-grey sky with slow internal gradation; the lion’s warm gold-ochre mane is the concentrated focal color; zebra stripes are black and bone-white on the same sculptural flank; deepest darks in the stripe bands, the eye, and the contact shadow, deep brown and blue-black. Fine description reserved for the lion’s eye and whiskers, the mane-hair-to-stripe join, and the change from paw to hoof; desert and sky remain quiet thin color layers. Wordless painting, no signature, no title, no second animal, no chase, no blood, no extra faces.
