A Salvador Dalí-inspired oil painting of a vast quiet desert in which a monumental apple is losing its rigidity and becoming still water. Precisely drawn transformed silhouettes, smooth sculptural tonal modeling, broad quiet painted planes. In the left-middle foreground sits a huge apple, still recognizable by a short woody stem, a small leaf, the calyx dimple, and warm red-to-gold skin; its lower-right volume has gone soft with continuous thickness, folds, and gravity, draping over itself, and that same contour lengthens into a shallow still lagoon spreading across the sand. The fruit-to-water change is one continuous form: the upper apple remains a solid rounded volume, then the skin thins into a glassy water surface that reflects the cool sky, with a clear shoreline where the lagoon meets ochre sand. A thin forked wooden crutch actually braces the apple's heaviest sag at a small contact point, bearing the weight of the softened fruit. Late-afternoon light from the left casts one long simple shadow of apple and crutch across the desert toward the right. Low distant horizon; empty ochre-and-bone plains recede with decreasing contrast and detail; a single small rock in the middle distance gives scale. Palette: large planes of ochre, yellow earth, and bone-white ground against a cool blue-grey sky with slow internal gradation; the apple's warm red-gold is the concentrated focal color; water is pale sky-blue with a few darker reflections; deepest darks sit in the contact shadow under the apple and the wood's shadow, deep brown and blue-black. Fine description reserved for the stem, the fruit-to-water join, the crutch contact, and the lagoon edge; desert and sky remain quiet thin color layers. Wordless painting, no signature, no title, no extra faces or unrelated objects.
