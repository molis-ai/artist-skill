Keep the same Salvador Dalí oil-painting language, desert, huge red-gold apple, cool sky, ochre ground, distant rock, wordless canvas, and 3:2 composition. Change only the object relationship: the apple and the water must become one continuous form, not a separate oval pool with a peel pointing at it. The apple’s lower-right volume itself spreads, thins, and flattens into the still lagoon, so the water is the continuation of the fruit: warm apple flesh grades into cool glassy water under the same light, with a shoreline that grows from the sag rather than sitting apart. The draping mass must keep continuous thickness and gravity as fruit-flesh, then become a thin sky-reflecting water sheet; do not use a discarded peel ribbon or dripping-juice effect. Move the thin forked wooden crutch so it actually braces the heaviest sag at a small contact point and bears the softened fruit’s weight; its foot may meet sand or the new water’s edge. Replace the two-prong electrical-plug shadow with a simple cast shadow that matches the real apple-and-drape silhouette. Keep stem, leaf, calyx, sculptural modeling, long late-afternoon shadow direction, and the quiet desert planes. No extra faces, no signature, no title.
