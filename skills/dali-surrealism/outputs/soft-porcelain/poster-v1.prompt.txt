Create one original Salvador Dalí-inspired surreal oil painting using his precise 1930s illusionistic visual language. Landscape 3:2, full artwork, no lettering, signature, frame or border. Theme: the fatigue that a resting object still cannot put down.

A single monumental ivory porcelain teacup, with one clearly recognizable oval rim and one handle, sits off-center on a low rough ochre stone ledge in an immense empty coastal plain. The cup has inexplicably lost its rigidity: its lower bowl drapes over the near edge like a heavy soft vessel, bending in a broad continuous fold while retaining a coherent porcelain wall and recognizable cup opening. The rim stays mostly round; the bend and the compressed contact on stone explain the weight. The loop handle stretches downward with the sagging body, still joined at both ends. A slender weathered forked wooden support touches the lowest drooping fold, a fragile prop for the cup's enormous weight. The porcelain remains opaque, softly luminous with tiny restrained glaze highlights and painted tonal variation, not melted liquid, translucent wax, rubber or chrome.

The cup occupies about half the width, the ledge is low, and much of the surrounding ground remains open. A distant thin cool blue sea and tiny ochre cliffs give the foreground object disquieting scale. The horizon is high enough for the ground to recede broadly. Warm raking light from the upper left models the cup and ledge; coherent long shadows stretch to the lower right and into open ground. The mood is silent, uneasy and still.

Paint with meticulous thin oil-color layers, controlled smooth modeling, selective crisp contours and tiny handmade irregularities. The warm bone-white cup, ochre stone and umber shadow mass are balanced by quiet blue-green distance and pale sky. Preserve distinct material character, subtle painted surface and atmospheric distance, without lens blur. Prioritize the physically readable fold, pressure and fragile support, not spectacle.
