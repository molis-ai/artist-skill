A Salvador Dalí 1930s–1946 hallucinatory oil painting. Precisely drawn transformed silhouettes, smooth sculptural tonal modeling, broad quiet painted planes.

A camel with spider-thin, extremely elongated legs stands in the foreground of a wide ochre plaza, in front of a pale Mediterranean hotel. The hotel remains a full building behind the camel: bone-white facade, arched loggia, quiet rectangular windows. The camel’s two humps echo the hotel’s arches—the same curve language, not a pasted face. The camel is slightly off-center, looking past the viewer; its bulk is large, its legs unnervingly fine, with clear hoof contacts and long shadows that reach toward the hotel.

Low horizon, plaza receding with slow tonal steps into cooler blue-grey air. Raking light from the left. The camel’s sculptural body is smoothly modeled; the hotel is simpler painted planes. Fine detail at the eye, nostrils, a few hairs, and the thin legs’ joints; walls and sky stay quiet.

Colors: ochre and yellow-earth plaza, bone-white hotel, camel tawny-stone, blue-grey distance, deep brown shadows. No people, no text, no signature, no extra clocks.
