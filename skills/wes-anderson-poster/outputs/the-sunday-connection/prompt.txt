Create one finished 2:3 vertical Wes Anderson-style cinematic movie poster, with the photographic realism of a meticulously art-directed full-scale film set. The theme is a church and a railway station sharing the daily life of a small European town. A composed, quietly poignant Sunday morning, circa 1965. Architecture and the connection between the two places are the visual protagonists.

CAMERA AND SPATIAL DESIGN
Look straight down the central pedestrian concourse of an open-ended railway terminus toward a church across a small stone-paved town square. A level camera, approximately adult chest height, a moderately wide natural photographic lens, straight verticals and one coherent central vanishing point. The station's high arched roof and paired columns form a grand, bilaterally balanced frame around the church. Matching columns on each side recede with consistent spacing and believable perspective. The church facade is precisely on the central axis and seen frontally through the large open end of the station: a complete bell tower topped by a small cross, an open belfry with a bronze bell, a round rose window, and a centered double wooden door. Leave clean daylight around its silhouette. The church must read as a substantial, clearly recognizable building, roughly one quarter of the image width and over one third of its height, never a tiny remote detail. Its narrower upper tower and wider lower facade have credible architectural proportions. The station roof, church and platform all occupy the same continuous real space, not a collage.

The side bays remain different in use. In the right bay a stopped vintage vermilion-red passenger railcar faces the camera on a track running parallel to the concourse; its rounded front, paired front windows, paired headlights, and buffers balance around the railcar's own vertical centerline. Show its whole front with believable wheel-to-rail support, a little of the carriage receding behind it, and a clear platform edge. The church is not on the railway tracks: the pedestrian central concourse leads to the square, while the railway track remains separated in the right side bay and ends at a modest buffer stop. In the left bay, an oak ticket counter, a wall-mounted analog station clock and an orderly luggage trolley establish the station's practical life. Preserve an unoccupied bench as a quiet interval. Do not mirror furnishings or copy the train onto the other side.

Two small, naturally proportioned adults establish scale without dominating the architecture: a station clerk in a dark blue wool uniform stands behind the left ticket counter looking down at a paper ticket; a woman in a muted blue dress stands near the central exit, her back in three-quarter view toward the camera, looking at the church, one hand resting on the handle of a small suitcase beside her. Show plausible hand contact, complete bodies, correct joints and grounded feet; a person is comfortably smaller than a doorway and the train front is substantially taller and wider than a person. No posed cast lineup. A generous continuous foreground of stone floor carries the typography while its joints and reflections continue into the scene.

A very light matte print reproduction unifies the finished poster but retains the photographed floor reflections and metal glints.
