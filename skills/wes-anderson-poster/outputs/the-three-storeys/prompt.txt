## 生成 2（image_gen，大厅视角）

Create one 2:3 vertical movie poster in Wes Anderson's live-action visual style: a formally staged 1930s grand-hotel lobby, with period art direction, deliberate color design and a gently photochemical finish.

Photograph from inside the hall, standing on the ground floor, looking straight on toward the far end of a triple-height atrium. This is a real interior, not a dollhouse and not a building with the front wall removed. The space is one tall room: pink plaster walls, a pale ceiling band left open for the title, and three stacked open galleries facing the hall. Each of the three floors has a complete horizontal railing spanning left to right — matching turned balusters and a continuous handrail on every level, including a low brass-and-wood rail around the ground-floor stair well. A grand staircase with a blue runner climbs through the center-back and actually lands on each gallery. The railings themselves show their own left-right structure from the front; furniture and people on the galleries are not mirrored.

Ground floor, behind the lowest railing: a hall porter in a long 1930s rose-brown coat, peaked cap and white gloves stands at the lobby’s inner doors to the right, one gloved hand holding a door leaf, looking toward the arriving guest, not at the camera. A civilian guest with a small suitcase has paused in the middle of the marble floor, looking down at a brass key. To the left, a concierge in a distinct blue uniform jacket stands behind a wooden reception desk, looking down at the open register, one hand on the page. An empty waiting chair sits beside the desk.

Second-floor gallery: one guest stands at the railing looking down into the hall, not at the camera. Third-floor gallery: the matching stretch of railing is empty. Keep people small against the tall hall: adults readable but not filling a storey. Four people only. They do not pose as a group.

Base the palette on GrandBudapest2. Assign dusty pink #E6A0C4 to the large lobby walls; pale lilac-blue #C6CDF7 to the ceiling and upper gallery walls; medium blue #7294D4 to the stair runner, door leaves, railing accents and the concierge jacket; warm dusty rose-brown #D8A499 to the porter’s coat, the desk and the wood of the railings. Derive a deep purple-blue ink from #7294D4 for the lettering. Reproduce these colors through a coherent vintage color-negative and print response: warm ivory highlights on plaster, marble and brass, gently cool blue in the stairwell, mellow pink walls and full but controlled blues. Soft interior lobby light from high windows, highlights rolled off smoothly, local texture contrast quieter than the large color fields. Wool, plaster, wood, brass, marble and carpet remain distinct. Fine integrated grain, no distressed paper, no fog.

Place the exact title "The Grand Lobby" in the upper ceiling band as one gently arched custom rounded-serif wordmark, about three quarters of the image width. Use plump, softly bracketed serifs, open counters, moderate stroke contrast, a short leftward swash from the T of The, and a modest descending tail from the y of Lobby. Maintain natural word spacing. The ink is that deep purple-blue, placed directly on the ceiling with no banner or box. Below it, set the exact subtitle "THE DOORMAN IS STILL HOLDING THE DOOR" in quiet, lightly spaced small sans-serif capitals, around one fifth of the main letter height. Comfortable clear interval above the third-floor railing. Flat ink, no shadow, no extra text, no hotel name, no credits.
