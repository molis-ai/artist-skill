Create one 3:4 Wes Anderson-style cinema poster: photographic subjects arranged in shallow pictorial space, reproduced as warm matte film-print key art. Unify the imagery and lettering with a harmonious vintage color grade, fine organic grain and a soft, finely textured matte surface.

The visual center is a young East Asian woman in her late twenties, standing with a vintage city bicycle in a Beijing imperial courtyard at the height of autumn. She looks directly into the camera with a composed, restrained expression and a deliberately posed, ceremonial stillness. She wears a camel wool coat with a rust-colored knitted scarf and dark brown lace-up shoes; one hand rests on the bicycle saddle, the other hangs quietly at her side. A small woven basket on the handlebars holds a single ripe orange persimmon, and one golden ginkgo leaf is caught on the left grip — the only slightly odd, observable detail. Place the woman and bicycle together in the lower-middle of the poster, occupying about two-fifths of the height, standing on pale cream flagstones dusted with fallen yellow leaves. Reserve the upper cream sky band, above the wall and between the tree crowns, for the title-and-subtitle group.

The single object spanning left and right is the long vermillion palace wall with a central rectangular gate and matching grey-tiled eave. Show its own structural symmetry from a straight-on frontal view, with matching brick courses, gate posts, door leaves, and eave lengths balanced around the wall’s vertical center axis; do not turn the wall into a three-quarter view. Preserve height and size differences among separate objects: a taller, fuller golden ginkgo tree at left of the gate and a slightly shorter, sparser ginkgo at right, both standing in front of the wall; the bicycle is lower and closer than the trees. Behind the wall, a few grey-tiled traditional rooftops and a pale cream autumn sky sit softly overlapping the eave line, with compressed perspective and no deep vanishing corridor.

Render the woman’s skin, wool coat, scarf knit, and the bicycle’s worn metal and leather with selective tactile cues and gentle volume. Keep the distant rooftops and upper wall softly defocused yet photographic. Give the flagstones and fallen leaves a few broad soft surface cues and short contact shadows where shoes and bicycle tires meet the ground. Use broad soft late-afternoon light from the upper left, restrained detail contrast, soft cream highlights and gently lifted compressed shadows. Blend fine visible organic film grain with a low-contrast matte photographic-print texture throughout the imagery and ink. Keep the finish softly tactile at ordinary viewing size, without sharp noise speckles, glossy digital edges or heavy distressed paper.

Choose terracotta-vermillion as the main hue on the large palace wall, and golden ginkgo ochre-yellow as the supporting hue on the two tree canopies and fallen leaves, with substantial visible areas for both. Soft cream ground in the sky and pale flagstones; deep burgundy-wine ink as the dark anchor for lettering and a few coat and bicycle darks. Give the colors a shared warm cream undertone. Apply a unified gentle film-print grade: restrain excessive chroma and local contrast while retaining vermillion warmth, ginkgo gold, and cool grey in the roof tiles. Integrate people, scenery and lettering into the same softly filtered palette, preserving skin identity and the coat’s camel color.

Main title, exactly: "Autumn in Peking". Design it as expressive typographic artwork using rounded vintage serif construction — plump arched bowls, soft bracketed serifs, medium stroke contrast, open counters — and a gentle arched composition echoing the gate’s top. Build even word spacing with slightly tighter pairing on "in". Draw a restrained swash from the left stem of the A, curling outward then back under the first letters, and a longer descending swash from the g in Peking that loops once toward the right end of the arch without crossing the woman’s head. Arrange the lettering along a shallow upward arch in the cream sky, reading left to right from Autumn toward Peking, with Autumn a little larger than Peking and "in" smaller and nested on the same curve. Give the complete title about the upper fifth of the poster, spanning most of the sky width, in deep burgundy-wine ink against the cream sky. Subtitle, exactly: "The Courtyard Turns to Gold", in quiet small-caps serif with thin even strokes, about one-fifth the title’s capital height, centered just below the arch and still in the cream sky, same burgundy-wine ink at lighter visual weight. Keep spelling, reading sequence and essential letter structure clear; the lettering may be visibly expressive rather than set on one straight baseline.
